Member & Customer Experience
CSS redesign: Houston market → #1 in Texas
Customer Satisfaction
Situation
As Field Manager in Houston, CSS scores led the city but hadn’t reached their ceiling. An audit of member feedback found the gap wasn’t repair quality — it was a communication breakdown at the first adjuster call.
Action
Led a three-part service redesign: created a “What Now” guide giving members a clear repair timeline; implemented mandatory milestone touchpoints so members never had to call for an update; coached adjusters on empathetic talking points tailored to members in crisis.
Result
#1 in Texas for CSS — plus a measurable drop in inbound inquiry volume, freeing adjusters for quality inspections.
Compliance & Financial Stewardship
Live review protocol: shifting from correction to prevention
Leakage Control
Situation
After taking over TX, CA, and OK virtual supplements, re-inspections were catching overwrites after payment had already issued — creating significant leakage risk and dragging compliance rankings.
Action
Implemented a two-pronged prevention model: each supervisor performed 20 “live reviews” per week — red-lining supplements with adjusters before the file was locked or paid — and data from those reviews fed directly into weekly coaching calibration sessions.
Result
#1 company-wide for compliance & accuracy — significant reduction in lost cost spend across three markets.
Paint invoice standard: stopping manufactured overcharges in California
Indemnity Protection
Situation
When launching the virtual supplement team in California, observed field management approving shop paint invoices generated through third-party services (e.g., EagleMMS). The invoices were manufactured — not independently verifiable — and were being approved largely out of fear of DOI complaints, leading to significant systemic overpayment.
Action
Rather than unilaterally stopping payments, worked with GEICO Legal to build a defensible position. Received approval to implement a new standard: payment only for verifiable materials. If the shop could not substantiate every line item, the company reverted to industry-standard paint calculation based on hours — not the proprietary invoice. Coached the team to hold that line professionally while preserving shop relationships.
Result
Major reduction in material overpayment — CA market — legally defensible, repeatable standard protecting indemnity spend without triggering DOI exposure.
Vendor & Partner Stewardship
DRP onboarding: diagnosing a cycle time gap through process analysis
DRP Partner Development
Situation
After opening a new DRP shop, data showed their cycle time was running a full day longer than comparable shops in the network. The performance gap was clear in the numbers — the root cause was not.
Action
Rather than presenting the data as a performance warning, met with the shop manager to work through it collaboratively. The conversation surfaced two distinct issues: (1) their intake process held all vehicles until a single designated technician ran pre-scans once per day, while higher-performing shops were scanning at the moment of customer drop-off — an immediate, recoverable delay built into their workflow; (2) upon reflection, recognized that while the performance metrics had been shared during onboarding, they had not been clearly explained — a communication gap on my part. Took ownership of that directly, walked through every key metric with the full shop team, and confirmed alignment before leaving.
Result
Cycle time gap closed within 30 days — shop realigned their intake sequence with network best practice and came into full performance alignment.
Copart transition: building a shared escalation system to close regional release time gaps
Enterprise Vendor Management
Situation
Following GEICO’s transition from IAA to Copart, overall vehicle retrieval times improved — but data revealed a significant inconsistency: certain areas of Houston were showing substantially longer release times than others. The problem wasn’t the vendor relationship at the top level; it was a gap in how individual adjusters were responding to Copart’s release requests, with some managing follow-ups well and others not at all.
Action
Met with Copart’s representative, Josh Speck, to analyze the release time data together and design a solution jointly rather than issuing a directive. Built a two-part system: (1) a structured escalation protocol where Copart’s standard adjuster contact (message + call) was supplemented by automatic supervisor notification after 2 days of no response, escalating further to the manager at 5 days — ensuring no release request could stall unnoticed regardless of individual adjuster follow-through; (2) authorized Copart a pre-approved spend threshold to cover pickup charges independently, removing a bottleneck that was causing delays when coordination was slow.
Result
Release times aligned across all Houston areas within 45 days — the escalation architecture removed individual variability from the process, and the spend authorization empowered Copart to act without waiting for adjuster response on routine pickups.

Identifying a productivity-quality gap through Tableau performance data
Operational Bottleneck
Situation
In late 2024, performance data revealed a 1st-quartile-productivity supervisor consistently landing in the 3rd quartile for re-inspection accuracy. The data said something in his process was broken — not his effort.
Action

Change Management & Innovation
Building the Virtual Supplement Team from zero
Organizational Change
Situation
Three years ago, was asked to design and launch a brand-new assign type — the Virtual Supplement Team for TX, CA, and OK. No playbook. No existing infrastructure. A significant shift from the field-based model the organization had always known.
Action
Used the ADKAR model to drive buy-in: shared data on why the virtual model would accelerate service; gave the team two months to map the field-to-virtual workflow gap; ran “mock days in the life” to build confidence before launch; established a daily data feedback loop. 22 associates voluntarily moved into the new department — maintaining cultural continuity through the ambiguity.
Result
#1 company-wide — three consecutive years — what started as an unknown became the organization’s benchmark program.
Talent Development & Culture
Turning a critic into a problem-solver through behavioral coaching
Difficult Feedback
Situation
A supervisor was consistently displaying complaint-driven behavior during leadership calls — critiquing new processes without alternatives, dragging team morale and meeting quality.
Action
Scheduled a direct 1-on-1 using behavioral-based coaching rather than accusation. Asked: “How do you feel your comments impacted the call?” The conversation uncovered the root cause — time management overwhelm. Responded on two fronts: collaborated on workload strategies to reduce stress, and set a clear meeting standard: every critique must be accompanied by at least one “art of the possible” solution.
Result
He became a lead voice in innovation sessions — and the leadership team aligned on a single forward-looking communication standard.
Talent selection framework
Team Building
Coachability
First filter: growth mindset. Candidates who see a file audit as a roadmap, not a critique. Technical skills can be trained — openness to feedback cannot.
Mission fit
Second filter: service heart. Does this person see a claim as a transaction, or as a member in crisis who needs an advocate?
Agility
Third filter: logical agility under pressure. Behavioral questions reveal how candidates handle conflicting data — shop estimate vs. market data — while staying objective and professionally composed.
